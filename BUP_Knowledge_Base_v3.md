# PART 1: University Identity


## 1.1 Overview

Bangladesh University of Professionals (BUP) is a public university administered under the regulatory framework of the Ministry of Defence and the Armed Forces of Bangladesh, established on June 5, 2008, pursuant to the Bangladesh University of Professionals Act, 2009 (Act No. 30 of 2009). The university is located at Mirpur Cantonment, Dhaka-1216, Bangladesh.

The university was founded with the statutory mandate to integrate, standardize, and elevate tertiary education across military training academies, staff colleges, specialized defense institutes, and civilian academic departments under a unified, internationally recognized academic administration.

Motto: Excellence Through Knowledge

Campus: Mirpur Cantonment, Dhaka-1216, Bangladesh

Type: Public University under Armed Forces administration / Ministry of Defence oversight

Accreditation: University Grants Commission (UGC) of Bangladesh, International Association of Universities (IAU), Association of Commonwealth Universities (ACU).


## 1.2 Vision, Mission & Core Values


### Vision

To be a leading university recognized globally and nationally for academic excellence, innovative research, integrity, and disciplined leadership, preparing graduates to excel in professional careers and national service.


### Mission

To provide cutting-edge, market-responsive higher education and professional training through modern pedagogical methods; foster pioneering interdisciplinary research; nurture ethical character, critical inquiry, and civic responsibility; and bridge military and civilian academic collaboration for sustainable socio-economic development.


### Core Values

1. Integrity & Ethics: Uncompromising adherence to ethical principles, moral accountability, and institutional transparency.

2. Discipline & Professionalism: Rigorous dedication to duty, academic punctuality, professional conduct, and mutual respect.

3. Academic Excellence & Innovation: Continuous striving for superior teaching, modern curricula, innovative problem solving, and technological integration.

4. Inclusivity & Diversity: Fostering an equitable academic environment open to civilian and armed forces learners across all socio-economic backgrounds.

5. Social Responsibility: Commitment to sustainable national development, humanitarian resilience, and public welfare.


### Strategic Objectives

• Develop internationally accredited undergraduate and graduate programs aligned with 4th Industrial Revolution (4IR) industry requirements.

• Promote high-impact research through competitive research grants, international indexed journal publications, and commercial patents.

• Strengthen academic infrastructure including smart classrooms, high-performance computing centers, and digital library archives.

• Facilitate global student and faculty mobility through partnerships with leading foreign universities.

Eligibility: Candidates must hold a 4-year Bachelor degree and Master degree in the relevant or allied discipline from a recognized university with minimum 2nd class or CGPA 3.00 on a 4.00 scale at both levels. Candidates with an International Baccalaureate (IB) Diploma must have completed a minimum of 24 points with at least 3 subjects at Higher Level (HL) and 3 subjects at Standard Level (SL). A point conversion certificate from the Ministry of Education / Inter-Board Standing Committee is mandatory.

## 6.1 FASS Masters Programs


### 6.1.1 Masters in Disaster Management & Resilience (MDMR)

Designed for practitioners dealing with natural and anthropogenic disasters. Curriculum focuses on hazard modeling, emergency supply chain logistics, risk assessment methodologies, community resilience, and climate adaptation frameworks. Total credits: 48 credits; Classes held on Fridays and Saturdays.


### 6.1.2 Master of Development Studies (Professional) — MDS

The Master of Development Studies (MDS) Professional program is one of BUP's flagship postgraduate programs, designed for development professionals, policy planners, civil servants, and NGO specialists. The program operates on a trimester system (3 trimesters per year) spanning 1.5 to 2 years, requiring 48 credits.

The curriculum includes core courses: MDS501 Development Studies in 21st Century: Theories and Approaches; MDS502 Urban Agenda in Development; MDS503 Society and Culture in Changing Landscape; MDS504 Design Thinking; MDS505 Economics of Development; MDS506 Power, Politics and Governance; MDS507 Research Methodology: Doing Development Research; MDS508 Result Based Management: Theory and Practice; MDS509 Study and Capstone Research Project. Classes are scheduled on Fridays and Saturdays.


### 6.1.3 Master of Arts in English Language Teaching & Applied Linguistics

Offers advanced pedagogical training in second language acquisition (SLA), syllabus design, language testing and evaluation, educational sociolinguistics, discourse analysis, and teacher training. Requires 36 credits across 3 semesters.


### 6.1.4 Master of Social Science in Economics

Offered in both Regular (1-year for 4-year BSS Economics graduates) and Professional tracks. Core study areas include Advanced Econometric Analysis, Time-Series Forecasting, Public Finance, Monetary Policy, and International Trade Economics.


### 6.1.5 MSS in Sociology | MSS in Disaster and Human Security Management

Focuses on contemporary sociological theories, social inequality, gender analysis, rural and urban transformations, human security frameworks, and quantitative/qualitative sociological research methodologies.


## 6.2 FSSS Masters Programs


### 6.2.1 Master of International Relations (MIR)

Explores multilateral diplomacy, regional security complexes, Indo-Pacific strategic rivalries, international political economy, crisis diplomacy, and strategic negotiations.


### 6.2.2 Master of Laws (LLM — Regular)

A 1-year academic master's degree (36 credits) for LLB (Hons) graduates. Specialization tracks include International Trade Law, Constitutional Law, Maritime and Admiralty Law, and Intellectual Property Rights.


### 6.2.3 Master of Laws (LLM — Professional)

A 1.5-year weekend postgraduate program (48 credits) designed for practicing legal advocates, corporate counsels, compliance officers, and judicial aspirants. Classes are scheduled on Fridays and Saturdays.


### 6.2.4 Master of Social Science in Mass Communication and Journalism

Offers advanced studies in media management, digital newsroom leadership, investigative reporting, multimedia documentary production, media law and ethics, crisis communication, and data journalism.


### 6.2.5 Master of Peace, Conflict and Human Rights Studies

Designed for peacebuilders, human rights officers, and humanitarian workers. Focuses on armed conflict analysis, conflict transformation, transitional justice, international human rights treaties, and human rights advocacy.

## 7.2 GPA and CGPA Calculation Formulas & Academic Standing

Grade Point Average (GPA) is computed for each semester, while Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) reflects overall performance across all completed semesters:

CGPA Formula: CGPA = (Sum of [Course Credits * Grade Point Earned]) / (Sum of Total Course Credits Attempted)

Academic Good Standing: Undergraduate students must maintain a minimum CGPA of 2.50 to remain in academic good standing. Postgraduate students must maintain a minimum CGPA of 2.75.

Academic Probation: A student whose CGPA falls below 2.50 at the end of any semester is automatically placed on Academic Probation. If the CGPA remains below 2.50 for two consecutive semesters, the student may be dismissed from the program or required to repeat the academic year pursuant to Academic Council approval.

Degree Graduation Minimum: To qualify for degree conferral, an undergraduate student must attain a minimum cumulative CGPA of 2.50 and have cleared all prescribed credit hours, mandatory internships, and comprehensive examinations.


## 7.3 Attendance Policy, Continuous Assessment & Collegiate/Non-Collegiate/Dis-Collegiate Rules

BUP enforces a strict attendance policy across all academic departments. Regular class participation is considered fundamental to academic discipline and professional competence.

Minimum Attendance Requirement: Students must attend a minimum of 75% of total classes held in each course during the semester to be categorized as 'Collegiate' and qualify unconditionally for semester final examinations.

Attendance Categorization:

• Collegiate (75% or above): Fully eligible to sit for semester final examinations without restriction.

• Non-Collegiate (60% to less than 75%): Permitted to sit for semester final examinations only upon payment of a prescribed Non-Collegiate fine per course and obtaining formal approval from the Dean upon recommendation of the Departmental Head.

• Dis-Collegiate (Less than 60%): Disqualified. Any student with less than 60% class attendance in a course is strictly barred from appearing in the semester final examination for that course. The student receives an 'F' grade in the course and must retake the course when offered next.

Continuous Evaluation Weightage Breakdown:

In undergraduate and regular graduate programs, courses are evaluated through a continuous assessment model:

• Class Attendance & Punctuality: 5% to 10%

• Class Tests / Unannounced Quizzes: 15% to 20%

• Mid-Term Examination: 20% to 25%

• Assignments, Case Studies, Term Papers & Presentations: 10% to 15%

• Semester Final Examination: 40% to 50%


## 7.4 Course Retake, Drop, and Grade Improvement Regulations

Course Retake for 'F' Grade: Any student who fails a course (grade 'F') must repeat/retake the course in full (including continuous assessments and final exam) at the earliest available semester. The new grade replaces the 'F' grade in CGPA computation, though the original 'F' remains recorded on the permanent transcript.

Grade Improvement Retake: A student earning a grade of 'C+' (2.50) or below may apply to retake the course once for grade improvement. A maximum of two courses per year may be retaken for improvement. The higher grade earned is incorporated into the CGPA calculation.

Course Drop Deadline: Students may formally drop a registered course within two weeks of semester commencement without academic penalty; dropped courses will not appear on the academic transcript.

## 8.3 Quotas and Admission Reservations

In compliance with national statutory policies and the university charter, BUP maintains designated quotas during undergraduate admissions:

1. Freedom Fighter Quota (FFQ): Allocated for children and grandchildren of certified Freedom Fighters of the 1971 Liberation War, verified against official Ministry of Liberation War Affairs gazettes and certificates.

2. Military & Defense Personnel Ward Quota: Designated seats for the children of serving and retired armed forces personnel (Bangladesh Army, Bangladesh Navy, Bangladesh Air Force) in recognition of institutional patronage.

3. Ethnic Minorities / Tribal Quota: Reserved seats for indigenous students from the Chittagong Hill Tracts and other tribal regions of Bangladesh.

4. Physically Challenged / Special Needs Quota: Allocations for candidates with verified physical disabilities.

Note: Candidates applying under any quota must achieve the minimum passing marks in the centralized written admission test to be eligible for quota seat allocation.


## 8.4 Scholarships, Merit Waivers & International / SAARC Student Reductions

Merit Scholarships: BUP awards semester-based merit tuition waivers to the top-performing students in each departmental batch (typically top 3% to 5% attaining a semester GPA of 3.85 or above).

Financial Need-Based Assistance: Economically underprivileged students exhibiting good academic progress can apply for partial tuition fee waivers through the Office of Student Affairs.

SAARC Student Tuition Subsidy: In accordance with SAARC cultural and educational exchange agreements, admitted students from SAARC member nations (India, Nepal, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Maldives, Pakistan, Afghanistan) receive substantial tuition fee waivers (up to 70% to 80% waiver on international student tuition surcharges), equating their educational expenses closely to domestic student rates.
